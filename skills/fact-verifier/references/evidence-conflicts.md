# Testimony and Conflicting Evidence

Read this when first-hand testimony, institutional statements, or other sources appear to disagree. These rules apply to the evidence for a particular proposition, not to a publisher's general reputation. The examples below are fictional decision examples, not a fact-check of any real observation.

## Establish whether the evidence conflicts

1. **Match the proposition.** Preserve quantifiers such as “ever,” “usually,” “only,” and “never.” Match observation time, place, instruments, lighting, population, and the distinction between detecting something and identifying it. A failure to observe on one occasion does not establish impossibility; one reliable exceptional observation can matter to an existential claim.
2. **Trace the testimony.** Locate the actual statement, speaker, date, context, and method of observation when available. Distinguish a first-hand account from someone repeating an unidentified person's story. A recording or transcript can establish what someone said without independently establishing that the reported event happened.
3. **Compare the evidential basis.** Assess specificity, contemporaneous records, opportunity to observe, identification checks, independent corroboration, and relevant uncertainty. Oral testimony is not automatically invalid without a photograph, and an official page may itself be summarizing testimony or using different conditions. Trace shared origins before treating those as independent evidence.
4. **Resolve or retain the disagreement.** Explain a concrete scope difference or evidential weakness when it resolves an apparent conflict. Do not dismiss contrary evidence merely because it is a minority view. If credible, applicable evidence still disagrees on a material premise, keep the question unresolved and describe both sides.

## Choose the verdict and stopping reason separately

| Evidence state                                                                                                                                                                                      | Decision                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reliable evidence supports the claim with its stated conditions; an opposing account concerns different conditions or lacks enough provenance to create a material countercase after investigation. | A directional verdict may be justified. Record the account and the reason it does not defeat the assessment, plus any residual limitation.                      |
| Reliable, applicable evidence directly contradicts the core proposition, and the plausible countercase has been examined without leaving a material conflict.                                       | Use `contradicted`; explain the evidence, not the publisher's authority or number of sources.                                                                   |
| Some facts hold, but omitted conditions demonstrably give a materially wrong impression.                                                                                                            | Use `misleading` and identify the omitted condition and its effect. This label is not a compromise for uncertain truth.                                         |
| Credible evidence addresses the same proposition and conditions but conflicts on a material premise that cannot be resolved.                                                                        | Use `verdict=unverified`, `stopReason=unresolved_conflict`, an unresolved question, and a corresponding material gap.                                           |
| The only relevant evidence is inadequately documented testimony, or necessary evidence cannot be inspected.                                                                                         | Use `unverified`. Choose the actual stopping reason, such as `source_unavailable` or `budget_exhausted`; do not invent a conflict between two adequate records. |

Confidence describes confidence in the assessment. Lowering it to `medium` or `low` does not make a directional verdict appropriate while a material conflict remains unresolved. Conversely, the mere existence of an unsupported objection does not force `unverified`. Investigate its relevance and provenance, then explain its treatment. Use `evidence_sufficient` only after the other sufficiency requirements, including a completed countersearch, are also met.

## Worked examples

- **Exceptional observation versus routine visibility.** An institutional guide says a fictional marker is not routinely identifiable from a ridge. A traceable observer reports identifying it during unusually clear conditions, with independently checked landmarks. These statements do not inherently conflict. The observation may support “can ever be identified under those conditions”; it does not establish “is routinely identifiable.” Preserve the original claim rather than silently changing its quantifier. Whether an unqualified statement is `misleading` requires evidence that the omitted conditions materially change its meaning.
- **An untraceable objection.** Inspected, applicable measurement records directly contradict a claimed height. A repost says an unnamed visitor remembers a different height, but a completed follow-up cannot locate an original account or establish matching date and measurement method. Retain the repost and the search limitation. It need not block `contradicted` if the inspected records resolve the claim; its weakness is a provenance and applicability finding, not a vote against a minority source.
- **A material testimonial conflict.** Two identifiable observers with comparable opportunities independently report opposite outcomes under the same stated conditions. Available records cannot explain the difference, and there is no decisive independent observation. Use `unverified` with `unresolved_conflict`; an institutional page repeating one account does not break the tie.
- **Missing conditions with an established effect.** A promotion accurately reports a fictional signal's detection in an experiment, then uses that real result to recommend a visitor experience while omitting the dedicated instrument and exceptional condition. The inspected record establishes the detection and explains why the visitor experience differs. `misleading` can describe that framing when the core occurrence is true and the wrong impression arises from those omissions. If the wording explicitly asserts “any visitor can detect it unaided at any time,” and applicable evidence rules out ordinary unaided detection, assess that proposition as `contradicted`. The distinction depends on what is asserted, not whether it appears in a headline or body text.
